The basis

The template compares every nutrient level per 1,000 kcal of metabolizable energy (ME), written per Mcal (for example g/Mcal), as in the NRC, AAFCO and FEDIAF tables. Its notes ask for diet values on the same basis. Three kinds of row use another basis: energy (kcal ME per day), energy density (kcal ME per g of dry matter, written kcal/g DM) and ratios (no unit).

This holds for every food and every selection. AAFCO compares a food below 4 kcal ME per g of dry matter with its dry-matter values, without correcting for energy. Under an AAFCO selection, when such a food's energy density is entered under Min, the template raises AAFCO's minimums (those a caloric restriction raises, and fat) to that dry-matter basis and still compares per Mcal (see The AAFCO dry matter factor).

On that basis, a diet's level for one nutrient is described by one of 14 bands, set by two numbers for that nutrient: its minimum (or 0 when there is none) and its range high. Some entries get another outcome instead (see Outcomes other than a band).

  • Four bands lie below the minimum, as shares of the minimum: under 25%, 25 to under 50%, 50 to under 75%, and 75 to under 100%.
  • Ten deciles divide the span from the minimum to the range high.

The 14 bands are the codes of Nutrient Decile Bands, carried in the Nutrient Decile (Common-Basis Expression) extension. The template's band labels are these codes' display strings, character for character.

The template offers 21 requirement selections for dogs and cats, for example NRC RA Dog Maintenance (NRC's recommended allowances for adult dogs at maintenance) or AAFCO Cat Growth and Reproduction. Every limit on this page is the one that applies under the selected requirement, whether the standard or the template sets it.

Inputs for one nutrient

For each nutrient, under the selected requirement, the template takes:

  • the minimum: the standard's, or one the template supplies, computes or adjusts (below), or none;
  • the maximum: the standard's maximum or safe upper limit, or one the template sets or computes (below), or none;
  • the multiplier K, used only when there is no maximum;
  • the diet value. The template takes up to two per row, entered under the headings Min and Max. It treats them alike except on rows with no minimum (see Rows with no minimum), for "none" (described as blank under Min and as "none" under Max), for protein: only protein entered under Min raises the arginine minimums (NRC and FEDIAF selections), and for energy density: only the energy density entered under Min sets the AAFCO dry matter factor.

From these it derives:

  • the base: the minimum, or 0 when there is no minimum;
  • the range high: the maximum when it is a number, whether the standard or the template set it; otherwise the minimum times K; otherwise 0, when there is neither a minimum nor a maximum.

A range high of the minimum times K is a derived-provisional ceiling: it prioritizes consistency and determinism, not toxicological calibration, and is revisable by the governance body.

  • No selection has an energy maximum, so the energy range high is always 1.55 times the energy minimum: the animal's daily energy requirement, or an entered intake that overrides it.
  • The Ca:P range high is 2 times the minimum only when the selection sets a Ca:P minimum but neither a Ca:P maximum nor a calcium maximum: in Version 21OCT26, the two AAFCO cat selections, whose Ca:P range is then 1 to 2. The 2 is the template's own choice, not an AAFCO maximum. Version 18OCT26 used 1, when no selection had a Ca:P minimum without a maximum.
  • With K = 1 and no maximum, an n-6:n-3 ratio row would have no range above its minimum. In Version 21OCT26 this does not occur: both rows always have a maximum.
  • The template's list of multipliers also gives 1 for water and 1.5 for energy density. Neither is used, because both rows have a fixed maximum (see Limits the template sets or computes).

Requirements per kg of metabolic body weight

Rows marked MBW (metabolic body weight) in the template come from requirements per kg BW^0.75 per day for dogs and per kg BW^0.67 per day for cats, where BW is the animal's body weight in kg. For adults the template asks for the optimal (lean) body weight, and for growing animals the current body weight; its note on cell A3 gives the standards' basis and one exception (NRC's equation for overweight cats, used with the current body weight). For the code version the provisional steward proposes the current body weight for every animal instead (see Energy rules proposed for the code version). The template converts both limits to per Mcal:

limit per Mcal = limit per kg BW^n per day × ((BW^n ÷ daily energy in kcal) × 1,000)

The daily energy is the template's calculated requirement for the selection and the animal, unless an intake is entered to override it. These per-Mcal limits therefore change with body weight and energy intake. Under the energy rules proposed for the code version, the minimums are converted with the energy fed and the maximums with the reference energy (see below). When an MBW row has no maximum, K is 4, or 7 for sodium.

Nine of the 21 selections have MBW values:

  • NRC RA Dog Maintenance, NRC RA Cat Maintenance, NRC RA Kitten Growth, NRC RA Cat Late Gestation and NRC RA Cat Peak Lactation, from NRC's values per kg BW^0.75 (dogs) or BW^0.67 (cats). NRC states its kitten and lactating queen values for one reference animal; the template uses them for every kitten or queen, as its own choice (see Maximum types for their maximums).
  • The four FEDIAF adult selections, from FEDIAF's minimums per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67 (Table VII-11), except fat and wet-diet selenium. Where the per-Mcal row has a maximum, the template gives the MBW row one too: the per-Mcal maximum times the selection's maintenance energy requirement (MER) ÷ 1,000, so the two maximums meet at the MER. Converted with an entered intake, these rows follow FEDIAF's own method for lower energy intakes.

The other twelve have none: the AAFCO selections, the FEDIAF growth and reproduction selections, NRC's two puppy selections and NRC RA Dog Late Gestation & Peak Lactation. For puppies and bitches NRC states its values per kg BW^0.75 for one reference animal only, and calculates other animals' needs from the per-Mcal values, as the template's per-Mcal rows do. Version 18OCT26 used those reference values for every puppy and bitch (see the errata on Template Status and Known Limitations).

Where the template sets or computes a limit (see Limits the template sets or computes), the MBW row follows the same rule on its own basis:

  • the arginine MBW minimum rises with the protein entered under Min on the protein MBW row, above that row's minimum;
  • the alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) MBW limits use the minimum and maximum of the linoleic acid (LA) MBW row, under NRC RA Dog Maintenance (the one NRC dog selection with MBW values);
  • the vitamin D2 MBW limits use the vitamin D MBW row;
  • the fixed medium-chain triglyceride maximum (17.568) and the taurine default (0.67) are per Mcal and are not converted.

Caloric restriction

An intentional caloric restriction can be entered as a percentage of the animal's energy need (cell G10): 25 means that the animal is fed 25% less energy than it needs. The template's cell rule accepts 0 (none) or a number from 1 to 55, typed without the % sign; a restriction below 1% is rejected as not clinically relevant. This percentage is the only way the template takes a restriction. When G10 holds a number above 0, the template multiplies the standard's per-Mcal minimums by 1 ÷ (1 − restriction ÷ 100), computing that factor first, so that the restricted intake still supplies the same daily amount. At 25%, each such minimum is multiplied by 4/3. Anything else in G10 adjusts nothing.

  • Not adjusted: the MBW rows (below), the energy minimum, energy density, fat, water, vitamin C, carbohydrate, the fibers, oxalate, purine and the standard's Ca:P minimum (the only ratio row with a minimum of its own). The template's note on fat gives its reason: fat per se is not essential (FEDIAF, AAFCO). The minimums of the essential fatty acids, ALA's own minimum among them, are adjusted.
  • When the percentage is entered without an intake in place of the calculated requirement (below), the energy minimum stays the calculated requirement. A planned restricted intake entered as the energy row's diet value therefore reads below it. The template's note on the energy minimum says how to compare the diet's energy with the planned intake instead: enter that intake in K4 as well as the percentage in G10. Whether a value below the energy minimum is deficient is open (see Open points, item 6).
  • The large-breed puppy calcium minimum, the larger of 3 g/Mcal and a body-weight term (0.54 g of calcium per kg of body weight a day, converted to g/Mcal with the daily energy; see Limits the template sets or computes), is adjusted too: the 3 always, and the body-weight term only when no intake is entered in place of the calculated requirement (below). Converted with such an intake, that term already supplies 0.54 g of calcium per kg of body weight a day at that intake. Its maximum (4.5, or 1.1 times the minimum when the minimum is above 4.5) follows it.
  • Maximums taken from a standard are not adjusted. Limits that the template computes from other rows use the adjusted minimums, and a range high of the minimum times K follows the adjusted minimum. So the ALA floor from the LA minimum, the calculated n-6:n-3 ratio limits and a Ca:P maximum calculated from the phosphorus minimum follow the restriction, and the calculated carbohydrate maximum falls as the protein minimum rises.
  • For arginine (NRC and FEDIAF selections), the standard's minimum is multiplied, and the increase for protein is then added, measured from the raised protein minimum. Under NRC RA Dog Maintenance, with 40 g/Mcal of protein entered under Min, the minimum is 1.03 g/Mcal with no restriction and 1.24 at 25%, not 1.03 × 4/3.
  • The template describes the restriction itself with the same band test, on a range of 0 to 55, or as "No restriction to describe" for 0, nothing entered or "none". That row describes an input, not a nutrient.
  • The template's notes recommend stating the restriction, and any intake entered in K4, wherever a band is cited, because the same g/Mcal can then fall in a different band.

The MBW rows come from separate requirements, per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67. The template's notes present the percentage as an alternative to them, and state that requirements on an MBW basis are separately protective. The percentage does not change them. They are converted with the daily energy the template uses: the calculated requirement, which the percentage does not reduce, or an intake entered in its place. Converted with an entered intake, an MBW row's minimum supplies the standard's daily amount at that intake. The energy rules proposed for the code version convert the MBW minimums with the energy fed instead, so a restriction raises them too, fat's excepted (see below).

An intake entered in place of the calculated requirement (cell K4, labelled "Overwrite & Use This Instead (kcal/day):") is not a restriction. Only a number above 0 in K4 counts as an entered intake, here and throughout this page; anything else leaves the calculated requirement in use. The template's cell rule on K4 accepts a number from 10 to 30,000 kcal/day. An entered intake is the daily energy that the template uses for the animal. It never triggers a restriction adjustment, because individual energy needs vary widely around the calculated average. The template's notes cite individual variation of up to 55% (the basis of its 55% cap), and about 50% for cats from the 2006 NRC. So an intake below the calculated requirement may meet the animal's whole need, or only part of it. Whether it is a restriction is for the user to say, by entering the percentage. For example, 1000 kcal/day entered where the calculated requirement is 1250 is not, by itself, a restriction. Entering 20 as the percentage as well says that 1000 kcal/day is 80% of the animal's need. Under the energy rules proposed for the code version, an intake below the reference energy also raises some minimums under the adult maintenance selections (see below).

  • An entered intake changes limits and bands only on the rows that the template converts with daily energy: the energy row (its minimum becomes the intake), the MBW rows (the ALA MBW row among them) and the large-breed puppy calcium row. Under the twelve selections with no MBW values it changes only the energy row, and under NRC RA Puppy 4-14 Wk Growth also the large-breed puppy calcium row, once a puppy body weight is entered for it (cell G65).
  • The template shows how far an entered intake is below the calculated requirement (cell N4, beside the label "Intake Below Calc. Req't:" in cell M4), and the summary sentences of the energy and MBW rows (column AR) quote it, for example "W/ An Energy Intake 20.00% Below The Calculated Energy Requirement". The figure is information only: it is not the percentage, which only the user enters, and no limit or band uses it.

Energy rules proposed for the code version

Proposed, pending ratification (provisional steward, 23 and 24 October 2026). The template is frozen and keeps the rules described above. For the code version, its successor and the reference since 24 October 2026, the provisional steward proposes the rules below in their place. They date from after the reviews of the template described in GOVERNANCE.md, and those reviewers have not reviewed them. Every difference between the code version and the template, rule by rule and note by note, is recorded in the code version's repository (docs/changes-from-21OCT26b.md).

Two energies set the limits:

  • Reference energy: the energy a standard's per-Mcal figures assume, at the current body weight: NRC's average adult dog, 130 × BW^0.75 kcal a day, for NRC RA Dog Maintenance and AAFCO Dog Maintenance; NRC's lean adult cat, 100 × BW^0.67, for NRC RA Cat Maintenance and AAFCO Cat Maintenance, whatever the cat type; FEDIAF's stated MER for the four FEDIAF adult selections; and for every other selection, the energy equation used for its life stage. AAFCO states no energy basis, so using NRC's for the AAFCO selections is the steward's proposal. The adult dog or cat type never changes the reference energy.
  • Energy fed: the intake entered, or else the calculated requirement (at the optimal body weight, when one is used) less any caloric restriction.

The rules:

  1. Body weight is the current body weight for every animal (for a lactating queen, her body weight at mating), not the optimal body weight that the template asks for in adults. This is a layer beyond the standards, complementary to them: their figures are based on growth or on a stable body weight, never on weight loss, which is often taken as a sign of nutrient deficiency rather than a desired outcome. When an animal is fed less to lose weight, the minimums rise so that its needs are still met. A caloric restriction is a cut from what the animal eats at its current weight, not from an equation's estimate, and back-calculating an optimal weight from body condition involves too much variability and calculation for clinical practice. An optional optimal body weight, for the adult maintenance selections only, says that the energy calculated at that weight is fed: when it is below the current body weight and no intake is entered, it sets the energy fed. It does not lower the energy minimum.
  2. MBW minimums are converted to per Mcal with the energy fed, so a restriction or an optimal body weight raises them too, and the diet still supplies the standard's daily amount per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67. Fat is the exception: neither fat row is raised by a restriction or an optimal body weight, also when the restriction is entered with an intake, because fat per se is not essential.
  3. MBW maximums are converted with the reference energy (or, where none can be calculated, as after week 4 of lactation, with the intake entered). They therefore do not change per Mcal with the adult type, an intake, a restriction or an optimal body weight, like the maximums a standard states per Mcal: the daily ceiling falls with the energy fed.
  4. The energy factor, reference energy ÷ energy fed, never below 1: under the eight adult maintenance selections, on a row with no MBW minimum (fat's and the ratios' excepted), the per-Mcal minimum is multiplied by the larger of the energy factor and the restriction factor. So an intake below the reference energy, or an adult type below NRC's average, raises these minimums, the AAFCO minimums among them (all but fat and the ratios), as the AAFCO selections have no MBW values. The template never raises the minimum of a per-Mcal row for an intake. Under the growth, gestation and lactation selections, where the animal is fed more energy than it needs to keep its weight stable and the extra goes to growth, a pregnancy or milk, nutrient needs rise and fall with the energy fed: the per-Mcal minimums hold at any intake, and only a restriction raises them.
  5. An energy fed below 10% of the reference energy is rejected as an input error.
  6. The choline maximum under the four NRC dog selections, NRC's presumed safe maximum of 500 mg/Mcal, is at least 1.1 times the higher of the choline minimum and the choline MBW minimum converted to per Mcal. A maximum raised this way is coded TPL, so a value above it is never excess. No standard sets a choline maximum from controlled safety data; calcium, whose tight control has data behind it, keeps the template's narrower rule: on the template's extra calcium row for NRC puppies 4 to 14 weeks old of a large expected size, the range high is 1.1 times that row's minimum only once the minimum is above NRC's 4.5 g/Mcal safe upper limit. The calcium row itself keeps 4.5 g/Mcal, and no selection's own calcium maximum, such as AAFCO's 4.5 g/Mcal for large-breed growth, ever rises.
  7. Ratios do not change with a restriction or a low energy fed, as a ratio's numerator and denominator rise together. Where a standard states no ratio, its range is computed from the other rows' minimums and maximums with the minimums before any restriction or energy factor (LA + AA : ALA + EPA + DHA and LA + AA : EPA + DHA under the NRC selections and FEDIAF Dog Early Growth & Reproduction, and the Ca:P maximum under the NRC puppy selections). The template computes them from the raised minimums, so a restriction narrows the range: at 30%, the NRC puppies' Ca:P maximum falls from 1.8 to 1.26.

Each result of the code version states the reference energy and its basis, the energy fed, the energy factor, and these rules in words.

Other rules proposed for the code version

Proposed, pending ratification (provisional steward, 24 October 2026; item 5, 25 October 2026). Like the energy rules above, these apply to the code version only; the frozen template keeps the rules described on this page. Each followed a test in which other AI models read the code version's results, and each is recorded in its changes document.

  1. AAFCO's maximums on dry matter. Under the AAFCO selections, for a food below 4 kcal/g DM with its energy density entered, AAFCO's maximums are, like its minimums, AAFCO's dry-matter values per Mcal of the food: the maximum per Mcal times the AAFCO dry matter factor (4 ÷ energy density). Ratios are not changed. They are authoritative, as AAFCO compares such a food on dry matter; the template keeps them per Mcal, and for the template this guide counts them as conditional (see The AAFCO dry matter factor and Maximum types).
  2. The large-breed puppy calcium row (NRC RA Puppy 4-14 Wk Growth) applies only to an expected mature body weight over 25 kg, as NRC's footnote h states, and its body-weight minimum uses the current body weight. The template applied it whenever its optional puppy body weight was entered.
  3. A nutrient's two rows. A value entered on one of a nutrient's per-Mcal and MBW rows is also checked on the other when that one is not entered: both take the diet's per-Mcal value. The template checked only the rows entered.
  4. NRC's fatty acid advice checks never count a nutrient that is not entered as 0: a check that needs one is not checked, and names it. A nutrient entered as one value counts with both the low and the high entries of the other; nutrients entered as ranges give the lowest and highest ratios they allow. The template counted a nutrient not entered as 0, and read the low entries together and the high entries together.
  5. Ratios computed from their parts. A ratio that the selection's standard sets (Ca : P under the AAFCO and FEDIAF selections; LA + AA : ALA + EPA + DHA under AAFCO's dog selections), not entered while every part of it is, is computed from the parts entered and checked as an entry. A part entered as one value counts on both sides; ranges give the lowest and highest ratios they allow; a numerator of 0 gives a ratio of 0, and a denominator of 0 or a part below 0 gives none. An entered ratio is checked as entered, with a warning when its parts give another. The template checked a ratio only as typed, so an inverted Ca : P went unreported when calcium and phosphorus each passed their own rows.

The AAFCO dry matter factor

The 2026 AAFCO profiles presume 4 kcal ME per g of dry matter and state that a food below that is not corrected for energy: AAFCO compares it with its dry-matter values. Under the five AAFCO selections, when the energy density entered under Min on the energy density row (cell G130) is a number above 0 and below 4 kcal/g DM, the template multiplies each minimum that a caloric restriction raises, and also the fat minimum, by 4 ÷ that energy density, the AAFCO dry matter factor (cell AS3). Otherwise the factor is 1: with nothing there, at 4 or more, and under the NRC and FEDIAF selections. At 3.6 kcal/g DM, for example, the adult cat protein minimum of 65 g/Mcal becomes 65 × 4 ÷ 3.6, about 72.2 g/Mcal, which is 26% protein in dry matter.

  • With a restriction as well, the two factors multiply on each minimum that both adjust: minimum = (dry matter factor × restriction factor) × the standard's minimum, where the dry matter factor is 4 ÷ energy density and the restriction factor is 1 ÷ (1 − restriction ÷ 100), each computed first. The fat minimum takes the dry matter factor alone.
  • Not multiplied: the energy minimum, energy density, water, vitamin C, carbohydrate, the fibers, oxalate, purine and the standard's Ca:P minimum. The AAFCO selections have no MBW values.
  • Limits computed from the multiplied minimums follow them: the vitamin D2 minimum for cats, the calculated n-6:n-3 ratio limits, and the calculated carbohydrate maximum, which falls as the protein and fat minimums rise.
  • Maximums taken from the standard are not changed. They stay per Mcal, the template's cautious choice, so a food at an AAFCO dry-matter maximum can read above the per-Mcal maximum here. While the factor is above 1, this guide counts AAFCO's maximums as conditional (see Maximum types). The code version raises them as it does the minimums (see Other rules proposed for the code version).
  • The multiplied minimum equals AAFCO's dry-matter value to within the rounding between AAFCO's two tables, except for 7 values that AAFCO prints differently in them: for cats, valine for growth and reproduction, and linoleic acid; for dogs, EPA + DHA, sodium, chloride and selenium for growth and reproduction, and selenium for adult maintenance (the template's note on cell C130).
  • Most summary sentences (column AR) then say so, for example "W/ An AAFCO Dry Matter Adjustment: Min x 1.143" at 3.5 kcal/g DM.
  • The template's cell rule on both energy density entries accepts a number from 2 to 9 kcal/g DM.

"Standard" means the selected standard's value; a standard's minimum is the adjusted one where a caloric restriction or the AAFCO dry matter factor adjusts it (above). A limit of none counts as 0 only where the two n-6:n-3 ratio rows add or divide limits, and in the ALA rules (the ALA maximum, and the test of whether the LA limit is above 0). In the ratio rows, AA is arachidonic acid, EPA eicosapentaenoic acid and DHA docosahexaenoic acid; EPA + DHA is a single row for their combined total. The ratio limits use the per-Mcal LA, AA, ALA and EPA + DHA rows, not their MBW rows.

Greater-than limits

NRC prints some safe upper limits as greater-than values, for example sodium for adult dogs, greater than 15 g/kg DM. The template enters such a maximum as the value plus 0.000000001 (here 3.750000001 g/Mcal), an addition its notes call clinically insignificant and far beyond any measurable precision. Its notes also state that at 7 decimal places this addition does not change how any value is described, and that a value equal to the limit is described as Highest (90% of Range or Greater) with or without it (see Open points, item 5). One such limit is entered without the addition: NRC's adult dog iodine limit, printed as at least 4 mg per kg DM. This guide counts NRC's greater-than limits as authoritative, except those per kg BW^0.67 that NRC states only for its reference kitten (see Maximum types).

Which band a value is in

Going up from the second band, the template checks whether the value is below that band's lower edge. The first time it is, the value is in the band before it. If it never is, the value is in range-d10. So:

  • Each band runs from its lower edge, included, up to the next band's lower edge, excluded. A value exactly on an edge is in the band that starts there, as the labels' "to <" wording says.
  • The first band takes every value below 25% of the minimum, negative values included (a carbohydrate by difference can be negative).
  • Unless the row's maximum is below its minimum (see A maximum below the minimum), the top band takes every value at or above its lower edge, including every value above the range high.
  • Where two edges are equal, the band between them is empty. On a row with no minimum, the 25 to 50, 50 to 75 and 75 to 100% bands are empty: a value of 0 or more goes straight to the deciles, and only a negative value is below them, in below-min-lt25.
  • The template does not compute a percentage of the range. A checker that does, and compares it with 10%, 20% and so on, can put a value very close to an edge in a different band.

Comparing at 7 decimal places

Every band test, and each comparison in the "No range" check, is made at 7 decimal places. The entered value and each band edge are both rounded to 7 decimal places before they are compared (the rounding is clinically insignificant and far beyond any measurable precision), so the result does not depend on how a spreadsheet or program handles the last binary digit of a computed edge. For example, the 80% calcium edge under NRC RA Dog Maintenance (1 to 4 g/Mcal) is 3.4, but the template computes it as ((4 − 1) × 0.8) + 1, which in binary floating point comes out as 3.4000000000000004; an exact comparison would place an entered 3.4 one band low.

The template's spreadsheet application rounds in two steps: it first takes the stored value to its decimal form with 15 significant digits, and then rounds that decimal to 7 places, half away from zero, negatives too. This matters only for a value or an edge that falls exactly halfway at the eighth decimal place: 0.99999995 against a minimum of 1 is range-d1. A program that rounds the stored binary value, just under 0.99999995, gets below-min-75-100 instead. So Python, Rust or any other code must round the same way, not the stored binary value, to give the template's bands (see Open points, item 8).

  • Other comparisons are not rounded: the no-minimum check, which compares an entry under Min with 0; the comparisons inside the limit formulas (for example the protein entered under Min against the protein minimum, for arginine); and the LA:ALA and EPA text checks. The DHA text check compares at 7 decimal places (see Open points, item 16).
  • This rounding is part of the comparison, not the formulation rounding that the guide leaves out of scope.
  • Displayed values are rounded too: band edges show two decimals, and minimums and maximums show their cells' formats. The comparison uses the computed limits, not the displayed ones. The template's own example: a minimum of 25.2307692 g/Mcal displays as 25.23, so an entered 25.23 is below the minimum and is in below-min-75-100.

Rows with no minimum

On a row with no minimum, the two entries are treated differently.

  • Under Max, when the row has a maximum, the deciles run from 0 to the maximum: a negative value is in below-min-lt25, and 0 is in range-d1. Under NRC RA Dog Maintenance this applies to taurine (maximum 0.67 g/Mcal), medium-chain triglycerides (17.568 g/Mcal) and water (1416 g/Mcal). When the row has no maximum either, the value is "No range" (see Rows with no range).
  • Under Min (on every such row but water), anything but "none" or a negative number gets the text "No min requirement &/or no min amount entered to describe" instead of a band, including nothing entered and an entry that is not a number. This check comes before the blank check, and the spreadsheet counts an empty entry as 0 and ranks text above every number. A negative value goes on to the "No range" test and the band test.

The water row describes only a value entered under Max. The template does not say which of the two entries a single diet value is, or what a below-minimum band means on a row with no minimum (see Open points).

Rows with no range

A value is described as "No range" instead of by a band when all three of these hold (the comparisons at 7 decimal places):

  1. the row has no maximum;
  2. its range high is not above the base;
  3. the row has no minimum, or the value is at or above the minimum.

A value below a stated minimum keeps its below-minimum band.

In Version 21OCT26, as long as the daily energy is above 0, every "No range" case is a row with neither a minimum nor a maximum, where the range high and the base are both 0. Under NRC RA Dog Maintenance, for example, the Ca:P ratio has neither limit, so a ratio of 1.2 entered under Max is described as "No range". On such a row, an entry under Min other than "none" or a negative number gets the no-minimum text instead, because that check comes first.

A maximum below the minimum

The template does not check that the range high is above the minimum. If a row's maximum is below its minimum, the row is not "No range", because it has a maximum. Its decile edges then run downward from the minimum: every value at or above the minimum is in range-d10, and values below the minimum keep their below-minimum bands.

This can happen when caloric restriction raises a minimum above a standard's maximum, when the AAFCO dry matter factor raises an AAFCO minimum above AAFCO's maximum, or when the computed carbohydrate maximum falls below its 25 g/Mcal minimum. The computed carbohydrate maximum does not go below 0 (since Version 21OCT26), and the fat minimum no longer rises with a restriction. At a 55% restriction the carbohydrate maximum is below 25 g/Mcal under one selection, FEDIAF Cat (MER 75 kcal/kg^0.67) (14.3 g/Mcal). With the dry matter factor and no restriction, it is below 25 g/Mcal under AAFCO Cat Maintenance below an energy density of about 2.06 kcal/g DM, and under AAFCO Cat Growth and Reproduction below about 2.23 kcal/g DM (0 at 2 kcal/g DM); with a 55% restriction as well, it is 0 under all five AAFCO selections at 2 kcal/g DM. These cases are listed for the governance body (see Conflicts for the governance body).

Open points

The template does not settle the points below. This page decides none of them in full: where one of its rules settles part of a point, the item points to that rule and lists what is still open.

  1. Recording "No range" and the other outcomes. Nutrient Decile Bands has no code for "No range", the no-minimum text, a blank, "none" or an error (see Outcomes other than a band). How a verdict records each of them is not stated.
  2. Which entry a diet value is. The template takes a value under Min and a value under Max on each row and describes each separately; its entry heading adds "(If No Req't Min, Put In Range)". It does not say which entry a single analysed or calculated diet value uses. The two differ on rows with no minimum, on the water row, on the protein rows (only the Min entry raises the arginine minimums) and on the energy density row (only the Min entry sets the AAFCO dry matter factor). When both are described, the row's summary sentence (column AR) joins the two descriptions with "to".
  3. A below-minimum band on a row with no minimum. A negative value on a row with no minimum but with a maximum, entered under Min or Max, is in below-min-lt25 although the row has no minimum (on a row with neither limit it is "No range"). What that band means there, and whether the value is deficient, is not stated.
  4. Which maximums are authoritative. Proposed, pending ratification: see Maximum types. The grouping follows the template's type codes. The template has no input for the conditions of conditional maximums (whether a nutrient is added as an additive, the animal's age, its breed), so none of them is checked (see Template Status and Known Limitations).
  5. Greater-than limits. Whether a verdict records an NRC greater-than limit as the printed value or with the template's added 0.000000001. Such a limit counts as authoritative, except one that NRC states only for its reference kitten (see Maximum types). The template's notes state that both give the same bands. Strictly, the addition shifts each decile edge by up to 0.0000000009 (times the conversion factor on an MBW row), so it can still move a rounded edge by 0.0000001 (a difference that is clinically insignificant and far beyond any measurable precision) when an edge lies that close to a rounding boundary, as caloric restriction or a large conversion factor can make it do; the notes' statement is a practical one.
  6. Minimums the template supplies or computes. The template supplies minimums where the selected standard gives none (vitamin C, carbohydrate, the fibers, oxalate, purine, energy density) and computes others (the energy minimum, the ALA floor from LA, vitamin D2, the two n-6:n-3 ratio rows). Its notes say that none of the supplied minimums is a requirement of the standards, and the oxalate and purine minimums come from human upper intake recommendations. The profile describes referenceRange.low as the requirement minimum, and this guide has no provenance flag for minimums. Whether a value below such a minimum is deficient, with or without a restriction, and whether such a minimum goes in referenceRange.low, is not stated. Nor is it stated for the 7 AAFCO values whose minimum, multiplied by the AAFCO dry matter factor, corresponds to a dry-matter value other than the one AAFCO prints (see The AAFCO dry matter factor). Raised standard minimums, minimums raised by the dry matter factor, arginine and the large-breed puppy calcium row are covered by the rules under Bands and the adequacy interpretation.
  7. Precision of deficient and excess. The template's descriptions have no test for "above the maximum"; its colouring compares the value with the range high at 7 decimal places (above it, only the range-high cell is coloured). Whether this guide's "below the minimum" and "above the maximum" tests use 7 decimal places is not stated. It matters for many raised minimums: at 25%, calcium at 1.3333333 g/Mcal under NRC RA Dog Maintenance is in range-d1, but below the exact minimum, 4/3.
  8. Rounding and arithmetic. Answered for the template (see Comparing at 7 decimal places): its spreadsheet application takes a value to its 15-significant-digit decimal form and rounds that, half away from zero, negatives too. The provisional steward checked this in a blank sheet of the same application on 19 October 2026: =ROUND(1.005,2) shows 1.01, =ROUND(-1.005,2) shows −1.01, and =ROUND(0.145*3,2) shows 0.44, although 0.145 × 3 is stored as 0.43499999999999994 (0.435000000000000 to 15 significant digits). Code that is to give the template's bands must round the same way, not the stored binary value. Still open: how the first step breaks an exact tie at the 15th significant digit (not checked), and the number type (binary floating point or decimal), as the template fixes the order of operations but not the type; after rounding to 7 decimal places these matter only at ties.
  9. Precision of recorded limits. The template compares with the computed limits (at 7 decimal places), not with the rounded figures it displays. A verdict's referenceRange can carry rounded ones (a minimum of 25.2307692 recorded as 25.23), which can change the band of a value near that limit.
  10. Invariants and validation. No formula checks that the minimum is below the range high, that values and limits are not negative, that the daily energy is above 0, or that K is above 0. The template's cell rules check some typed inputs: the selection, the type of dog and the type of cat (lists), the caloric restriction (0, or a number from 1 to 55), the life stage (a list), the week of lactation (a whole number from 0 to 7) and the number of puppies nursing (a whole number from 0 to 20). Besides the caloric restriction, life-stage, lactation week and puppy count rules, the template's cell rules reject text and numbers outside set ranges in body weight (0.05 to 150 kg), expected mature body weight (0.5 to 150 kg), an entered intake (10 to 30,000 kcal/day), the puppy body weight for the large-breed calcium row (0.5 to 50 kg) and energy density entered under Min or Max (2 to 9 kcal/g DM), and they reject text in the other Min and Max entries of the nutrient rows. A rule checks only what is typed into its own cell: not a blank, not a value that reaches the cell another way (a paste can replace the cell's rule), and not a combination of inputs. Under the growth selections that use the expected mature body weight, a body weight above it gives the message "Expected Mature BW Must Be At Least Current BW" instead of an energy; without that message the daily energy would be 0 or negative above 1.88 times the expected mature body weight under FEDIAF's puppy equation, 2.20 times under NRC's kitten equation and 2.65 times under NRC's puppy equation. When a maximum falls below the minimum, a value between the two is both below the minimum and above the maximum. Where a caloric restriction or the AAFCO dry matter factor raises a standard's minimum above its maximum, such a value is deficient (see Bands and the adequacy interpretation); in other cases, such as carbohydrate, no rule says which interpretation applies. Whether any rule on this page becomes a validator-checked invariant or stays in narrative is not decided.
  11. Several reference ranges for one nutrient. The template bands each row on its own: per-Mcal and MBW rows; general rows and rows for foods of more than 12% moisture (taurine, copper, selenium); calcium and the large-breed puppy calcium row; vitamin D and vitamin D2, whose combined total the template says it does not compute; retinol for cats and vitamin A for dogs. Two cases follow from the rules under Bands and the adequacy interpretation: a value below a standard's minimum raised for a caloric restriction is deficient even if it meets the nutrient's MBW row, which the restriction does not raise, and a value below the large-breed puppy calcium minimum is deficient even if it meets the general calcium row. Otherwise, which row decides a verdict is not stated, nor whether a component may carry more than one referenceRange, or which range maxProvenance then describes: FHIR reads several reference ranges as alternatives, so in the two cases above a second range from the MBW or general calcium row would read as met.
  12. Inputs a verdict needs. The limits depend on the selection, the caloric restriction, body weight (for adults, the optimal body weight), the type of dog or cat, an intake entered in place of the calculated requirement, the expected mature body weight, the life stage, the week of lactation, the number of puppies nursing, the puppy body weight for the large-breed calcium row, the protein entered under Min (for arginine) and, under the AAFCO selections, the energy density entered under Min (for the AAFCO dry matter factor). The profile records the standard and its edition (item 13), the life stage and the caloric restriction, as a percentage (see Bands and the adequacy interpretation), but none of the other inputs, including an entered intake and the energy density. While the energy equation lacks an input, the template shows a message instead of the energy minimum (see Outcomes other than a band) and defines no fallback.
  13. Selections and editions. The 21 selections do not map one to one onto this guide's Requirement Standards and Life Stages codes: for example FEDIAF adult dogs at a maintenance energy requirement (MER) of 95 or 110 kcal/kg^0.75, NRC puppies 4 to 14 weeks old or 14 weeks and older, AAFCO small-and-medium or large-breed growth, and NRC cat late gestation or peak lactation. The NRC selections use NRC's recommended allowances, except where the template's notes say otherwise (for example choline under the four NRC dog selections, which use NRC's adequate intake of 340 mg/Mcal; 2006 NRC page 233). The template cites the 2006 NRC, the 2026 AAFCO Official Publication and FEDIAF's September 2025 guidelines. The Requirement Standards code system has a code for each of these editions (NRC-2006, AAFCO-2026 and FEDIAF-2025), under the code of its body, so a verdict can record the edition it was checked against. Which selection within a standard and life stage a verdict used is not recorded.
  14. Converting diet values to the basis. The template compares per Mcal and has no conversion from a diet's dry-matter or as-fed values to per Mcal. Its converter tab converts one amount at a time, by hand, the other way: from per Mcal to per kg of dry matter, for an entered energy density.
  15. Limits outside the template's columns. Limits that exist only on a dry-matter basis, for a product category or for part of a nutrient have no column: for example AAFCO's calcium maximum of 1.8% DM for some all-life-stages dog foods, AAFCO's limit on phosphorus from soluble sources for cats (the template's note on cell B67 of the Standards tab), and the limits on added selenium (see Template Status and Known Limitations).
  16. Text checks. The template's LA:ALA and EPA share checks, and its check that DHA (EPA + DHA minus EPA) meets NRC's minimum for adult cats (NRC's EPA + DHA value there is DHA only), return text, not bands. The LA:ALA and EPA checks compare without rounding; the DHA check compares at 7 decimal places. Whether these checks are part of a verdict is not stated.
  17. Ratio nutrients. The template bands the Ca:P and the two n-6:n-3 ratio rows like any other row. The profile includes ratio nutrients such as Ca:P as components and excludes non-linear ratio nutrients, but neither the profile nor the template says which ratios are non-linear, so whether the two n-6:n-3 ratio rows are in scope is not stated.
  18. Coverage. Species other than dogs and cats, and nutrients outside the template's rows.
  19. Overall verdict. The profile's top-level interpretation is the overall complete-and-balanced verdict. How it follows from the per-nutrient results, and from the outcomes in item 1, is not stated.
  20. ALA under caloric restriction. Settled in Version 21OCT26: a caloric restriction raises ALA's own per-Mcal minimum like the other minimums, before it is compared with the floor from LA under the four NRC dog selections (see Caloric restriction). Under those four, whether a value below the floor is deficient is item 6.

Conflicts for the governance body

The standards bodies could not foresee every use of their figures. Where applying a stated rule gives conflicting results in some use case, the template and this page apply the rule as decided, and the conflict is listed here for the governance body to refine or resolve. The examples use the template's default inputs (body weight 15 kg for dogs) unless stated.

  1. NRC's safe upper limits per kg BW^0.75 and per Mcal. Both are authoritative, but a limit per kg BW^0.75 converted with an animal's energy differs from the per-Mcal one for animals far from NRC's average. Under NRC RA Dog Maintenance, the fat limit per kg BW^0.75 converts to 54 g/Mcal for a dog of the Great Dane type (200 kcal per kg BW^0.75), below NRC's 82.5 g/Mcal, and to 113.7 g/Mcal for an inactive dog (95 kcal per kg BW^0.75).
  2. NRC's choline maximum and its source. NRC (page 233) proposes a presumed safe maximum of 2,000 mg choline per kg diet, which the template uses as 500 mg/Mcal. The study NRC cites, McKibbin et al. (1945), fed growing puppies 2,000 mg of choline chloride per kg of ration as fed: about 1,490 mg choline per kg as fed, or 373 mg/Mcal. The template follows NRC's print and states the inconsistency in its note.
  3. NRC's reference-animal values. NRC states its values per kg BW^0.67 for kittens and lactating queens, and per kg BW^0.75 for puppies and lactating bitches, for one reference animal each. The template uses the kitten and queen values for every kitten or queen (their maximums conditional), and none for puppies and bitches.
  4. FEDIAF's minimums per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67 and its main tables. FEDIAF's Table VII-11 is rounded and partly disagrees with its per-Mcal tables. At the MER, the converted minimum per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67 is up to 9% above the per-Mcal minimum for dogs (sodium under FEDIAF Dog (MER 95 kcal/kg^0.75): 0.3158 against 0.29 g/Mcal) and up to 23% for cats (histidine under FEDIAF Cat (MER 75 kcal/kg^0.67): 1.07 against 0.87 g/Mcal), so a diet exactly at FEDIAF's per-Mcal minimum can read below the MBW minimum.
  5. The FEDIAF maximums per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67. The template derives them from FEDIAF's per-Mcal maximums at the MER. With an intake in K4 away from the MER the two rows disagree: above the MER the MBW maximum is below FEDIAF's per-Mcal maximum, and below the MER above it. Under FEDIAF Cat (MER 100 kcal/kg^0.67), with an intake of 130 kcal per kg BW^0.67, the vitamin D MBW maximum is 5,769 against FEDIAF's 7,500 IU/Mcal.
  6. FEDIAF's wet-diet selenium values. The template uses FEDIAF's dry-diet selenium value per kg BW^0.75 or BW^0.67 for every food. With an intake in K4 below the MER, a food of more than 12% moisture can then be asked for up to about 19% (dogs) or 20% (cats) less selenium than FEDIAF's wet-diet value: 65 against 80 mcg/Mcal for a dog under FEDIAF Dog (MER 110 kcal/kg^0.75) fed 80 kcal per kg BW^0.75.
  7. AAFCO's dry-matter rule. AAFCO compares a food below 4 kcal/g DM with its dry-matter values; the template raises the minimums and keeps the maximums per Mcal, and this guide counts AAFCO's maximums as conditional while the factor applies. For 7 values AAFCO's two tables disagree (see The AAFCO dry matter factor).
  8. FEDIAF growth maximums used for reproduction. FEDIAF's Table VII-18a gives cat maximums for growth and reproduction without naming a stage, but its Table III-4b labels the arginine, methionine, tryptophan and Ca:P maximums "Growth". The template uses them under FEDIAF Cat Reproduction, coded N* (conditional).
  9. Minimums above maximums under a caloric restriction. The restriction raises minimums but not maximums, so from about 30% some minimums pass a maximum: FEDIAF's legal selenium maximum under the two FEDIAF dog growth selections (above 29.6%), the choline maximum under the four NRC dog selections (above 32%), the calcium maximum under the two NRC puppy selections and AAFCO Large Breed Dog Growth (above 33.3%), and above 37.5% the phosphorus maximum under the two AAFCO dog growth selections and the calcium maximum under FEDIAF Dog Early Growth (< 14 wk) & Reproduction, among others up to 55%. Under the energy rules proposed for the code version, the choline maximum rises with the minimum and is no longer passed.
  10. Minimums above maximums under the AAFCO dry matter factor. With no restriction, AAFCO Large Breed Dog Growth's calcium minimum passes its maximum below 2⅔ kcal/g DM, about 2.67 (3 × 4 ÷ energy density against 4.5 g/Mcal), and the two AAFCO dog growth selections' phosphorus minimum passes its maximum below 2.5 kcal/g DM. The calculated carbohydrate maximum falls below its 25 g/Mcal minimum under AAFCO Cat Maintenance (below about 2.06 kcal/g DM) and AAFCO Cat Growth and Reproduction (below about 2.23 kcal/g DM, and 0 at 2 kcal/g DM), and it is 0 under all five AAFCO selections at 2 kcal/g DM with a 55% restriction. A restriction adds further cases.
  11. The choline MBW minimum and the per-Mcal maximum. Under NRC RA Dog Maintenance, the choline minimum per kg BW^0.75, converted with an intake entered in K4 below 90 kcal per kg BW^0.75, is above the 500 mg/Mcal maximum. Under the energy rules proposed for the code version, a restriction or an optimal body weight can bring the energy fed that low too, and the maximum rises to at least 1.1 times the minimum, so a diet can meet both rows.
  12. NRC's large-breed puppy calcium rule and its calcium safe upper limit. For puppies of more than 25 kg expected mature body weight, up to 14 weeks of age, NRC asks at least 0.54 g of calcium per kg of body weight a day (Table 15-3, footnote h); at a low energy intake this is more than NRC's 4.5 g/Mcal safe upper limit. Under NRC RA Puppy 4-14 Wk Growth, a 20 kg puppy eating 1,324 kcal a day needs 8.16 g/Mcal on the large-breed puppy calcium row (maximum 8.97, 1.1 times the minimum), while the general calcium row's maximum is NRC's 4.5 g/Mcal, so a diet that meets the first row is excess on the second.
